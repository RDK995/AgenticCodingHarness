### Decisions

- **The batching instruction was removed on its own evidence.** It was the half of
  §42 expected to matter: at 70.4% fixed cost, cutting turns 30% saves ~21%. It
  produced no measurable turn reduction — 22 → 22 on `06`; on `05` the orchestrator
  fell 54 → 49 but the driving session rose 22 → 27 and +136k tokens, and `SKILL.md`
  was untouched by B19, so that movement is run-to-run variance and the −5 cannot be
  credited to the instruction either. It cost ~50 tokens per turn, ~2.4k per
  milestone, indefinitely. Paying a certain cost for an unmeasurable benefit is the
  failure §42 exists to attack, so it went.

  The turn lever is real in the arithmetic. An instruction is not a mechanism for
  pulling it, and no replacement mechanism is proposed here.

- **The diagnosis outlived the fix.** Agent prose explains only 27% of the
  orchestrator's baseline gap over the worker — 2,508 tokens of 9,135. The rest is
  system prompt and tool definitions: the worker declares 6 tools, the orchestrator
  is unrestricted by design. The largest per-turn constant is therefore unreachable
  by editing instructions, and no future deletion pass should be expected to pay
  much.

- **Per-task context resets are rejected on the measurement.** They attack only the
  29.6% growth while re-paying an ~18k baseline per reset, and no per-task durable
  record exists to resume from. Milestone sizing remains the task-level control.

- **Honest scale.** B19 delivers 1-4% depending on fixture, against the "cut turns
  30%, save 21%" reasoning used to propose it. Recorded rather than restated as a
  success.

### Follow-ups

- **Tool-definition surface is the untouched constant.** It is the larger half of
  the orchestrator's baseline and nothing here addresses it. The orchestrator's
  unrestricted tool set is deliberate — it keeps the risky work — so this is a real
  trade rather than an oversight, and worth measuring before anyone assumes it can
  be narrowed.
- **Turn count still has the headroom.** ~21% of spend for a 30% reduction, with no
  known mechanism. Worth revisiting only with a mechanism and a way to measure it
  across more than one paired sample; single runs of a nondeterministic agent cannot
  resolve differences this small.
