### Validation — fixture 06 after the batching removal

Passes. 16 genuine checks PASS; the 2 failures are C5/C6, the **waived** ladder
checks. C6 had previously "passed" as a false positive — the grep matched the word
*escalate* inside "did not escalate the tier". This run phrases it differently, so
the false positive is gone and the check now fails honestly. Not a regression: the
report still states why the ladder was not spent — "Implemented by the orchestrator
directly (not delegated): the crux of this milestone is an unsatisfiable
requirement, not a coding task" — proves the impossibility, and leaves
`test_split.py` byte-identical.

**Total context is not a usable measure on this fixture. Four samples:**

| `06` run | orchestrator.md | batching text | Turns | Context | Baseline | Ctx/turn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B18 | 422 lines | absent | 22 | 501,870 | 19,545 | 22,812 |
| B19a | 353 lines | present | 22 | 480,309 | 18,842 | 21,832 |
| B19b | 353 lines | absent | 28 | 636,533 | 18,778 | 22,733 |
| B19c | 353 lines | absent | 24 | 540,390 | 18,618 | 22,516 |

B19b and B19c are the *same commit on the same fixture*. Turns swing 22 → 28
(27%) and total context 33%. That spread dwarfs anything B19 changed, and it
retrospectively invalidates the single-sample figures quoted earlier in this
milestone — the "−4.3%" on `06` and "−0.6%" on `05` were each one paired sample
inside a ±33% band. They were reported as results; they were noise.

**What is stable is what B19 actually claims:**

- **Baseline — 5.0% spread, and it tracks the file.** 19,545 before the trim;
  18,842 / 18,778 / 18,618 after. Mean reduction **−799 tokens/turn**, with every
  post-trim run below the pre-trim one.
- **Context per turn — 22,812 / 21,832 / 22,733 / 22,516**, standard deviation 386
  on a ~22.5k mean. Effectively constant.

The model is `cost ≈ (baseline + accumulated) × turns`: the per-turn cost is
stable and predictable, and **turn count is the volatile term nobody controls**.
B19 shaved the stable term. It did not touch the volatile one.

This vindicates removing the batching instruction twice over: it showed no
effect, and no single-run comparison could have detected an effect of that size
anyway.

### Decisions

- **The batching instruction was removed on its own evidence.** It was the half of
  §42 expected to matter: at 70.4% fixed cost, cutting turns 30% saves ~21%. It
  produced no measurable turn reduction — 22 → 22 on `06`; on `05` the orchestrator
  fell 54 → 49 but the driving session rose 22 → 27 and +136k tokens, and `SKILL.md`
  was untouched by B19, so that movement is run-to-run variance and the −5 cannot be
  credited to the instruction either. It cost ~50 tokens per turn, ~2.4k per
  milestone, indefinitely. Paying a certain cost for an unmeasurable benefit is the
  failure §42 exists to attack, so it went.

  The turn lever is real in the arithmetic. An instruction is not a mechanism for
  pulling it, and no replacement mechanism is proposed here.

- **The diagnosis outlived the fix.** Agent prose explains only 27% of the
  orchestrator's baseline gap over the worker — 2,508 tokens of 9,135. The rest is
  system prompt and tool definitions: the worker declares 6 tools, the orchestrator
  is unrestricted by design. The largest per-turn constant is therefore unreachable
  by editing instructions, and no future deletion pass should be expected to pay
  much.

- **Per-task context resets are rejected on the measurement.** They attack only the
  29.6% growth while re-paying an ~18k baseline per reset, and no per-task durable
  record exists to resume from. Milestone sizing remains the task-level control.

- **Honest scale.** B19 delivers 1-4% depending on fixture, against the "cut turns
  30%, save 21%" reasoning used to propose it. Recorded rather than restated as a
  success.

### Follow-ups

- **Tool-definition surface is the untouched constant.** It is the larger half of
  the orchestrator's baseline and nothing here addresses it. The orchestrator's
  unrestricted tool set is deliberate — it keeps the risky work — so this is a real
  trade rather than an oversight, and worth measuring before anyone assumes it can
  be narrowed.
- **Turn count still has the headroom, and cannot be measured the way B19 tried.**
  ~21% of spend for a 30% reduction, with no known mechanism. Four samples of `06`
  put run-to-run turn variance at 27% on an identical task, so any future mechanism
  aimed at turn count **cannot be validated by paired fixture runs** — it needs
  repeated sampling with a stated sample size. Worth knowing before anyone tries.
